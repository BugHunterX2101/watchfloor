Watchfloor privacy policy

Last updated October 15, 2026

Watchfloor is an Alexa+ add-on that tells sales teams which deals in their pipeline are at risk and why, and acts on them by voice only after the user confirms. It is a demonstration built for the Amazon Alexa+ and AWS hackathon. Every company, deal and person in its workspace is fictional.

What Watchfloor receives

  • Your Watchfloor sign-in. When you link Watchfloor in the Alexa app, you sign in with a Watchfloor account (an email address and a password). Passwords are stored only as an Argon2id hash.
  • Your requests, as text. Alexa sends Watchfloor the tool requests it derives from what you say, such as "explain the risk on this deal". Watchfloor never receives your voice recordings; Amazon handles speech under its own privacy notice.
  • What you ask it to save. Notes and field changes you log on a deal, standing orders, briefing preferences, and follow-up tasks you confirm.

How it is used

Only to answer your requests: score and explain pipeline risk, draft follow-ups, and carry out the actions you confirm. Watchfloor does not sell data, show advertising or build profiles.

Who processes it

  • Amazon Web Services hosts Watchfloor and its database in the US East (N. Virginia) Region.
  • Google (Gemini API) generates spoken answers, deep reviews and follow-up drafts from the deal data involved, while Amazon Bedrock is unavailable to this deployment. On Google's free tier, Google may use that content to improve its products, which is one reason the workspace holds only fictional data.
  • Slack receives a message only when a workspace has connected a Slack channel and you confirm an alert or a review is posted there.

Records and security

Every tool call is written to an audit log with its time, the tool, the outcome and a SHA-256 hash of its arguments, never the arguments themselves. Access tokens expire within an hour; refresh tokens rotate on every use and are stored only as hashes. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.

Retention and your choices

You can unlink Watchfloor at any time in the Alexa app, and Alexa then stops using its tokens; access tokens expire within an hour on their own. This demonstration, its accounts and its data will be deleted after hackathon judging ends in November 2026.
